Flood Safety Guide — India

Before, during and after a flood
112
National emergency number — police, fire, ambulance and flood rescue

Before a flood

  • Know your area's flood risk and the location of the nearest higher ground or shelter
  • Prepare an emergency kit: torch, radio, power bank, drinking water (3 days), dry food, medicines, first aid, documents in a waterproof bag
  • Keep 112 and your district control room number saved and written on paper
  • Charge phones whenever heavy rain is forecast; keep a charged power bank
  • If you live near a river, stream or low-lying area, be ready to move early — do not wait for water to arrive

During a flood

  • Never walk or drive through floodwater — six inches of moving water can knock an adult down; two feet can float a car
  • Avoid swollen rivers, drains, culverts and bridges; most flood deaths happen near them
  • Stay away from electricity poles, transformers and fallen wires — electrocution is a leading flood hazard
  • Do not use lifts in waterlogged buildings; use stairs
  • If water enters your home, switch off the main electricity and move to the highest floor or roof, and call 112
  • Follow district administration advisories and evacuation orders without delay
  • Keep children away from floodwater — open drains and sharp debris are hidden underwater

After a flood

  • Return home only when authorities say it is safe
  • Do not drink floodwater or use it for cooking — boil drinking water; water-borne diseases (cholera, typhoid, leptospirosis) spike after floods
  • Do not touch electrical appliances that were submerged until an electrician checks them
  • Discard food and medicines that came into contact with floodwater
  • Disinfect floors and utensils; watch for snakes and insects that move into dry homes
  • Report unsafe structures, open drains and damaged roads to your ward office or district administration
